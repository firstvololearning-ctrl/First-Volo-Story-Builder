📖 About First Volo Story Builder

First Volo Story Builder is an interactive narrative-language tool for generating story ideas, organizing narrative relationships, and practicing specific language targets. It can support oral, written, typed, or scribed story production depending on the instructional goal.

Welcome Story Instructional Support Planner Save Print Tips

👋 Welcome

Students generate visual story parts and decide how those parts fit together into a coherent story. The cards provide content to work with; they do not supply the causal or narrative relationships for the student.

Story Builder can be used for oral storytelling, planning, written or typed composition, or scribed composition. If handwriting, spelling, or typing is not the instructional target, an educator can scribe the student's language so those demands do not replace the language task.

🎲 Build a Story

Roll all story elements at once or roll individual cards to change only one part of the story.

  • Character
  • Setting
  • Problem
  • Feeling
  • Action / Attempt
  • Resolution

The Item card is optional. It can add an object as an extra creative constraint, but Item is not treated as a required narrative function.

Thousands of different story combinations are possible.

⚙️ Customize Story Elements

Educators can show or hide story parts to match student needs. The core sequence includes Character, Setting, Problem, Feeling, Action / Attempt, and Resolution. Item is available separately as an optional story element.

🎯 Instructional Support

Educators can begin with a known instructional target when it comes from an established goal or prior assessment, or choose Observe First and listen to the student's first whole-story attempt before selecting the primary target. Story Builder does not diagnose the target or score student performance.

When an Instructional Target is selected, Story Builder uses a Whole → Part → Whole teaching cycle. The student first tells or generates the complete story before targeted help opens. The educator then uses the Story Planner to work only on the selected target where a breakdown appears, reconnects the repaired language to the surrounding story, and finally asks the student to tell the complete story again with targeted support closed.

Within the Work on Target stage, the student attempts the language first. If targeted help is needed, choose Need help? to reveal one support step at a time in the relevant part of the Story Planner. Support becomes more explicit only as needed, and the student then retries or reformulates the same language demand.

Current instructional targets include Story Organization, Connections & Cohesion, Cause & Effect, Sentence Formulation, Elaboration, Perspective & Internal State, and Vocabulary Precision.

Access help such as clarifying directions, locating a story element, rereading a prompt, navigating the planner, or explaining a non-target word is kept separate from support for the selected instructional target.

🗂 Interactive Story Planner

The Story Planner automatically copies selected story elements into a graphic organizer where students can develop the relationships and language that make the story work. It can support oral rehearsal, brief notes, scribed responses, or written planning.

  • Character
  • Setting
  • Problem
  • Feeling
  • Action / Attempt
  • Resolution

If the optional Item element is turned on, it also appears in the planner so the student can decide whether and how it matters to the story.

Guiding questions remain available as the planning baseline. When an Instructional Target is selected, targeted support can be opened one step at a time only where it is needed.

During a later retell or Tell Again, the educator may allow the student to refer to the Story Planner as a scaffold whether or not anything has been written in it. The planner may function through its story images and organization alone, through brief or scribed notes, or through student writing. Hide the planner when an unsupported whole-story tell is the goal.

💾 Save & Open Stories

Story Builder automatically saves work entered in the Story Planner and writing area in the browser.

My Stories lets you keep multiple Story Builder projects in this browser on this device. Once a story is added to My Stories, later edits to that story are updated automatically.

Cloud Sync is optional. Sign in from My Stories to make saved stories available across signed-in devices. Story Builder continues keeping local copies so My Stories can still be used when the internet is unavailable.

Download Backup creates a separate Story Builder file for backup or transfer. To use that file later, return to Story Builder and choose Restore Backup.

Downloaded copies preserve:

  • Story title
  • Writing
  • Planner notes
  • Story selections
  • Display and vocabulary preferences
  • Instructional Target, Whole → Part → Whole stage, and targeted-support state

💡 Tips for Educators

Match the task to the instructional target

  • The student can tell the story aloud, type or write it, or dictate while an educator scribes.
  • If handwriting, spelling, or typing is not the target, do not require those skills to be the main way the student demonstrates narrative language.
  • Use only the story elements that are useful for the student and the instructional goal. Item remains optional.

Use targeted support responsively

  • Let the student attempt the language before opening targeted support.
  • Use the least support likely to help, then have the student retry the same language demand.
  • Reduce or remove support when the student can maintain the target with less help.
  • Provide access help when needed without treating it as support for the language target.

Look for transfer

  • After the student improves within one story, use a new story with the same target to see whether the skill carries over to new content.
