Story Builder User Guide

Use visual ideas to support the learner’s own story construction, then teach a specific language target when needed.

Choose what you are doing

  • Work together: tell or type in the same workspace. Use the optional writing area if the learner wants to type or the educator wants to write down what they say. Give prompts aloud or show them on the shared screen. Use Story Goals, the teaching cycle, planner and optional picture ideas. Rolling may help with ideas, but is never required first.
  • Set up independent practice: follow Choose a student, Choose a Story Goal, and Choose optional help to set up their own workspace. There are no story-rolling controls in this setup task.
  • Student sign-in: the student opens their own workspace for independent practice using the supports you selected ahead of time. The shared teaching controls apply to the workspace you are using together.

The two educator tasks share your educator access. Switching tasks preserves the current teaching draft; it does not sign in as a student or automatically save changed student options. Use Save supports and check the confirmation when changing a student’s settings.

Work together on one shared screen if you want to see the learner’s writing as they type. The learner’s independent practice draft does not appear on this teaching screen. You can still open the teaching prompts here and give them aloud while looking at the learner’s screen. Those prompts do not appear on the learner’s screen.

Choose story elements and picture ideas

In Story elements & picture ideas, choose the visible story parts and select Learner’s own ideas (no supplied pictures), Picture ideas, or Both. The learner creates the story in every option. Show picture labels adds written words to supplied pictures and is available only when pictures are used. Your teaching choice is saved with the story project.

In Set up independent practice, separately choose the story ideas available to that student and whether picture labels are shown. Use Save supports to save those settings for their signed-in workspace. This does not change your current teaching story.

Use picture ideas when helpful

Start with the learner’s own story ideas, or use picture ideas whenever helpful. Under Story elements & picture ideas, open Picture ideas (optional) to choose pictures; rolling is never a required first step. Choose visible story elements as needed. The learner develops the Plan, Action / Attempt(s) and Resolution. A Plan Idea card provides optional help. Item is an optional creative constraint. Use oral storytelling, writing, typing or educator scribing according to the task.

Explore Thinking (optional)

Beside Feeling in the Story Planner, open Explore Thinking when a character’s understanding matters to the story. Thinking is what a character believes, knows, remembers or suspects; a Plan is what the character intends to do.

Start with “What might the character be thinking here?” Open Thinking help for prompts about knowledge, beliefs and differences between characters. Ask what in the story supports the idea. A belief can be mistaken, thinking can stay unchanged, and “We don’t know” is a valid response.

Students may use “[Name] thinks that…”, “[Name] knows…” or “[Name] wonders whether…”, or use their own words. Thinking notes save with planner work. Choose Add this thought to My Story to insert the note at the writing cursor, then read and edit it in the whole story. Thinking is optional; it adds no required story step and does not automatically enter the completed story.

Choose a Story Goal

Choose an established target before the first tell, or use Observe First and choose after listening. The available targets are Story Organization, Connections & Cohesion, Cause & Effect, Sentence Formulation, Elaboration, and Perspective & Internal State.

Choose between Story Organization and Connections & Cohesion

Story Organization is about which story parts the listener needs and where they belong. If the learner says Maya searched every bench but leaves out what she lost, ask “What was Maya looking for?” Help the learner decide where to add that information.

Connections & Cohesion is about making it clear how two ideas fit or who a word like “she” refers to. If two characters were searching at the same time, ask “Were they looking at the same time?” If needed, model “While Maya looked under the bench, Leo checked the swings.” Use a time word when it helps express the learner’s meaning.

Choose Cause & Effect when the skill is explaining why something happened or what it led to. Ask about one specific gap, offer a model using the learner’s details if needed, then invite a retry and return to the whole story.

Use the teaching cycle

The cycle appears above the picture cards. Its next-action button helps you move between First Tell, Work on Target and Tell Again. Open Educator guidance for more detail. Use Work together for the shared teaching interface, including optional picture ideas. Use Set up independent practice to select a student’s goal and available help for their own sign-in; story-rolling controls are absent from that task.

  1. First Tell: listen to the complete story before adding targeted help.
  2. Work on Target: allow an attempt, open support only as needed, and ask for a retry. Reconnect the improved idea to the story.
  3. Tell Again: return to the complete story with the short Story Goal in mind. Choose whether the planner stays visible.

The Yes / Sometimes / Not yet reflection is a student self-check, not an automatic performance score. Try a new story to examine use of the target beyond the practiced story.

The three-stage overview at the top is navigation. Complete the current story activity below it, then use the next-step button after the work area. Tell Again follows targeted practice and a retry. Oral storytelling does not require written answers.

During Work on Target, keep the student’s Story Goal visible. Open the single Educator guidance dropdown for directions; then use the target supports in the planner only as needed.

Rate the same skill across both tells

Research informs Story Builder’s teaching approach. The prompts and rating choices are Story Builder design decisions. Use the ratings to describe what you observed for the selected skill during the session.

After First Tell, choose a Story Goal if you started with Observe First. Under Educator skill rating, choose one skill to rate in both tells. You can record an optional rating and evidence note before moving on.

During Work on Target, use only the prompts the learner needs. Select Mark as given when you actually give a prompt. You can undo a mark.

After Tell Again, rate the same skill alongside the First Tell rating. Use Independent, With support, Not yet, or Not observed. Leave the rating blank if you haven’t rated it. Open Session summary and choose Copy summary to copy both ratings, evidence, and the prompts you marked. The checklist and marks save with this story draft.

Examples of what to listen for
Story GoalWhat you might rate
Story OrganizationDid the learner introduce the main character, make the problem clear, put events where the listener could follow them, or tell how it ended?
Connections & CohesionCould the listener tell who “she” meant or whether two events happened together?
Cause & EffectDid the learner explain why something happened or what changed because of it?
Sentence FormulationDid the learner express an idea in a complete sentence or put two related ideas into one clear sentence?
ElaborationDid the learner add a detail that helped the listener picture a person, place, or action?
Perspective & Internal StateDid the learner say what a character thought, felt, knew, or wanted, or use a clue in the story to support an interpretation?

Choose a skill that fits the learner

The Target + Goal + Evidence Builder offers guidance for Grades 2–3, 4–5, and 6–8. The teaching screen uses the same six Story Goals across these bands. Start with what you hear in the learner’s story and adjust the complexity of the skill and the amount of help. For example, begin with one clear reason for an event, then look for a clearer explanation with less help as the learner gains experience.

When a learner tells or writes a story, ask what the listener or reader needs to understand. If “Who do you mean by ‘she’?” is too open, offer a choice between characters already in the story or model a sentence with the intended name. Then invite the learner to try that part again.

Use targeted help

Support opens through three prompts: Look / Listen, Think and A Clue. Ask a specific question about the part the learner needs help with. Give them time to respond. Offer a model using their story details when needed, then invite a retry. Use Next question or clue for another prompt to say to the learner about the same skill. Coaching Ideas opens guidance for you as the educator. You can open it right away. Sentence Help and Coaching Ideas are separate choices: use either first, both, or neither. Return to an earlier prompt when useful, ask for a retry in the learner’s own words, then reconnect the idea to the story.

For Sentence Formulation, choose whether the learner is building a sentence or combining ideas. Select the intended relationship and level of grammatical support. Suggested structures are possibilities: preserve the learner’s meaning and use educator judgment. Do not require a successful ending, a learned lesson or a causal link that the story does not support.

How saving works

Cloud Sync keeps your educator “My Stories” library available across devices. Saving to a learner keeps selected teaching-cycle records and evidence. These are separate saves. Syncing a story does not attach it to a learner, and saving learner evidence does not save the complete story to My Stories.

Saving learner evidence

Use Save cycle title & story cards for that metadata and the evidence controls for each narrative version. A written snapshot captures the writing at that point. Choosing the oral option records that the story was told aloud; it does not record audio or create a transcript. Resolve the required stages before completing the cycle. Completed evidence is read-only.

For example, save a reusable story project in My Stories and use Cloud Sync to reopen it on another device. Separately save the learner’s First Tell and Tell Again evidence in their cycle. Editing the working draft later does not automatically replace those saved snapshots.

Printing and privacy

Open Print, resources & more. Print Student Planner uses the current story elements, pictures, labels, work, and Story Goal. Print Educator Prompts & Scoring gives you prompts and separate First Tell and Tell Again ratings. With Observe First, write in the goal after listening. Choose Print Both for the planner and educator sheet together. Color and grayscale planners and an anchor chart are also available. Use fictional names and details in stories and made-up learner labels. Keep exported and printed records secure and follow your organization’s approved procedures.
