## Critical product-grounding rule

The grade bands in the educator Target + Goal + Evidence Builder **do not represent three different Story Builder runtimes**. The current app uses one target/support engine. Grade band changes the educator-facing objective/developmental expectation only.

Current in-app phases:

**First Tell → Work on Target → Tell Again**

Story Goal selection is a separate educator control. **Observe First · Choose After First Tell** is the default.

The educator can open **Look / Listen**, **Think**, and **A Clue**. **Sentence starters & examples** and **Coaching ideas for educators** offer optional support. The educator can mark prompts actually given and compare ratings for the same skill across First Tell and Tell Again. During Tell Again, targeted help stays closed and the planner may be available for reference.

Adapt wait time, questions, and modeling to the learner. This informs prompt wording and access to optional help. |
| [Pico et al. (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1044/2021_LSHSS-20-00160) | The review found positive effects of narrative intervention. | [Hessling & Schuele (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1044/2019_LSHSS-19-00082) | Individualized story elements and separate personal narrative probes inform a distinct follow-up with a different story. Two of four participants showed a functional relation in the Story Champs study. |
| [Gillam et al. (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1044/2017_LSHSS-17-0047) | Early evidence for a specific program addressing story structure and language within stories supports keeping both kinds of goals available. The study does not establish oral-to-written transfer. |
| [Meyer et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1044/2025_LSHSS-24-00065) | Observer feedback improved procedure use in three clinicians. This informs the separation of clinician actions, such as prompts given, from learner performance ratings. |

## Prompt and workflow review

The wording review keeps the six teaching goals and Whole → Part → Whole. It uses concrete questions and optional help available from the first prompt. Clinicians can use a model before moving through more questions. The learner retries using their own story meaning. Prompts given remain separate from the two learner ratings. No story must end successfully, include a learned lesson, or express a cause the learner did not mean.

The grouped goal picker retains all six goals. A separate optional new-story observation is available after Tell Again; learner reports compare the same skill across up to 50 recent saved cycles. These features are design decisions, not validated measures.

Research informs Story Builder’s teaching approach. The prompts and rating choices are Story Builder design decisions. Use the ratings to describe what you observed for the selected skill during the session. The app’s prompts and ratings require separate evaluation from the named interventions and measures discussed in the sources.

It concerns secondary academic writing. The full tutorial confirms the examples shown in the screenshots: Table 1 (p. 45) gives prompts for observing planning, putting ideas into words, and reviewing. Table 3 informs flexible support. Spencer & Petersen remains the direct source for the narrative-specific question and optional model.

- **Story Organization:** Which story part does the listener need and where does it belong?
- **Connections & Cohesion:** Can the listener identify who or what a word refers to, or tell how events fit in time?
- **Cause & Effect:** Can the learner explain a reason or a consequence that fits the story?
- **Sentence Formulation:** Can the learner express an idea in a complete sentence or combine related ideas while preserving their meaning?
- **Elaboration:** Does one added detail help the listener picture a person, place, or action?
- **Perspective & Internal State:** Can the learner tell what a character thinks or feels, or point to a story detail that supports a possible interpretation? Identifying who is narrating is a separate skill.

The six teaching goals each offer specific rating choices. The educator selects one criterion and uses it for both tells. The choices and ratings describe session observations. The academic writing examples in the ASHA notes help frame questions about clarity for an audience. They do not validate the narrative rating choices. Choose a question or a model that fits the learner and note help given separately from performance.

The guide and teaching prompts apply this with a reference example. Ask who “she” means, offer a choice among characters already in the story, or model a sentence with the intended name. The learner then tries the part again. Grade bands guide the complexity and amount of support, while the teaching screen uses the same six goals and rating choices.

## Current product scope

The educator teaching cycle offers six Story Goals. Vocabulary Precision is also represented in the broader target and student support tools.

- **Story Organization:** Include and place the important story parts so the listener can follow the whole story. Sequencing belongs here when the clinician is helping arrange the story.
- **Connections & Cohesion:** Express how ideas relate and make references clear. Time words belong here when they communicate intended timing, such as two events happening at once.
- **Cause & Effect:** Explain a supported reason or consequence. A time relationship alone does not establish a cause.

### Story Organization

**Current product construct:** Organize the important parts of the story into a coherent narrative: who/where → problem and feeling → plan → action/attempt → resolution.

**Current Student Mode support types:** Question prompt, Story-part reminder, Sentence starter.

**Current Story Builder implementation:**
- First Tell captures the complete story before the Planner or targeted support is opened.
- Work on Target focuses on the selected story function where repair is needed.
- After Retry, the repaired part is reconnected to the surrounding story before Tell Again.

### Connections & Cohesion

**Current product construct:** Connect ideas and events rather than produce isolated statements, using clear relationships, sequencing, connectors, and referents.

**Current Student Mode support types:** Question prompt, Connecting words, Sentence starter.

**Current Story Builder implementation:**
- Connections & Cohesion support is available across the Story Planner categories.
- Current built-in language includes temporal/causal connectors such as when, while, after, because, so, but, suddenly, finally, and as a result when they fit the intended meaning.
- The current product watches for unclear referents, but it does not contain a dedicated pronoun/referent-repair module.

### Cause & Effect

**Current product construct:** Explain why important events or feelings occur, how events and intended goals relate to a plan, and what happens because of the character’s attempt.

**Current Student Mode support types:** Question prompt, Connecting words, Sentence starter.

**Current Story Builder implementation:**
- Current Cause & Effect support targets problem, feeling, plan, attempt, optional item, and resolution relationships.
- Built-in prompts include because, so, finally, as a result, to, and in order to when they fit the intended relationship.
- The current product does not include a dedicated multi-step causal-chain, competing-cause, or counterfactual module.

### Sentence Formulation

**Current product construct:** Turn an intended idea into a complete, organized spoken or written sentence.

**Current Student Mode support types:** Question prompt, Sentence reminder, Sentence starter.

**Current Story Builder implementation:**
- Sentence Formulation begins with the student’s intended idea rather than a new clinician-generated sentence.
- The current built-in reminder is Who? → did what? → what or whom? → where or why?, with story-part sentence starters.
- Both building a sentence and combining related ideas are available, with relationship and grammatical-support choices. These are flexible supports, not a validated syntax hierarchy.

### Elaboration

**Current product construct:** Add useful information that clarifies or develops an important story idea or event.

**Current Student Mode support types:** Question prompt, Detail reminder, Sentence starter.

**Current Story Builder implementation:**
- Current Elaboration prompts ask for one useful detail tied to the selected story part.
- The program explicitly distinguishes useful detail from extra information that does not help the important story idea.
- The current product does not include dedicated dialogue, pacing, reflection, or literary-craft modules.

### Perspective & Internal State

**Current product construct:** Explain what characters feel, think, know, want, expect, wonder, or intend and connect those ideas to story events when appropriate.

**Current Student Mode support types:** Question prompt, Words to try, Sentence starter.

**Current Story Builder implementation:**
- Current Perspective & Internal State prompts target character, problem, feeling, plan, attempt, and resolution.
- Built-in word support includes wants, knows, thinks, hopes, wonders, expects, planned, decided, realized, learned, and understood.
- The current product does not include a dedicated two-character perspective-comparison or systematic perspective-change module.

### Vocabulary Precision

**Current product construct:** Choose words that communicate the intended meaning more specifically.

**Current Student Mode support types:** Question prompt, Word reminder, Sentence starter.

**Current Story Builder implementation:**
- Current Vocabulary Precision support works inside the student’s narrative rather than as a decontextualized vocabulary lesson.
- The built-in prompt asks How? What kind? How strong? What exactly happened? and supports comparison among candidate words.
- The current product does not include dedicated connotation, register, tone, or broad vocabulary-intervention modules.

## Explicit current product gaps — DO NOT silently turn these into generated Story Builder capabilities

- No dedicated advanced multi-episode/plot-architecture module.
- No dedicated pronoun/referent-repair scaffold, although unclear referents are monitored under Connections & Cohesion.
- No dedicated multi-step causal-chain, competing-cause, or counterfactual causal-reasoning module.
- No systematic sentence-combining or comprehensive older-student syntax hierarchy.
- No dedicated dialogue, pacing, reflection, or literary-craft module.
- No dedicated two-character perspective-comparison, false-belief, or systematic perspective-change module.
- No dedicated connotation/register/tone or broad decontextualized vocabulary module.
